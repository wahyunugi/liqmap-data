What you are looking at

Heatmap (behind the candles): estimated liquidation levels at each moment, from the repo's model v6, a replica of CoinGlass Model 1. Each column shows the levels created during the window before it that price has not touched yet. Brighter = more USD.

Window: 24h is the model checked against CoinGlass. 3D and 7D use the same rules but keep levels longer, so zones stay visible on 4h and daily candles; they are not checked against CoinGlass. Auto picks 24h up to 1h candles, 3D for 4h, 7D for 1D. When zoomed out, neighbouring price boxes are added together so each row stays readable.

Liquidation map (bars on the right): the same levels for the candle under your cursor, or the latest candle, as a side profile. blue = long positions that get liquidated if price falls to that level; orange = short positions liquidated if price rises to it. Longer bar = more USD.

Lower panes

Actual liquidations: recorded on Binance (Coinalyze feed). Shorts liquidated plot upward, longs liquidated downward. Undercounts the real total, because Binance publishes only the largest liquidation per coin per second.

Open interest: the total size of futures positions still open on Binance, in coins (each contract has a buyer and a seller and is counted once). Rising = new positions opening; falling = positions closing or being liquidated. The heatmap is built from its increases.

Volume: coins traded per candle.

Per candle, on every interval: volume and actual liquidations are totals over the candle; open interest is the value at the candle's close; the heatmap column and the liquidation map show the levels still open at the candle's close (not a sum over the candle).

Data and limits

The heatmap shows where new money entered, not where liquidations are guaranteed. It assumes 25x/50x/100x leverage and isolated margin. Times are WIB (UTC+7); candle buckets follow Binance (UTC).

Source: repo wahyunugi/liqmap-data. The site rebuilds itself after each data collection (every hour) and shows the last days.
