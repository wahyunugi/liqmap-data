The ratio is the number of Binance accounts holding a long position for every account holding a short one (Binance's "long/short account ratio", all accounts). It counts accounts, not money: a $100 account counts the same as a $10 million one. Every contract has a buyer and a seller, so when 70% of accounts are long, the 30% that are short hold on average more than twice as much each; a high ratio means many small accounts long against fewer, bigger accounts short. The chart pane "Long vs short" shows it per candle (purple), next to the same ratio for Binance's top 20% accounts by margin balance, measured by position size (yellow). The table splits every day since the data starts into five equal groups by the day's average ratio, then looks at the price 30 days later. The 30-day windows overlap, so neighbouring days are not independent results, and each group holds only around a dozen truly separate 30-day periods. Read it as how things have tended to go, not as a forecast. Source: Binance public archive (5-minute values, up to yesterday) and Coinalyze (today).

US spot ETF flows

Range
FundNet flow

Net money into (above zero) or out of (below zero) the US spot ETFs each trading day, all funds added together. Spot ETFs (BlackRock IBIT, Fidelity FBTC, and others) must hold real coins, so an inflow means the funds bought that much and an outflow means they sold. Hover the chart to see any day by fund. Source: Farside Investors daily recap, checked hourly. Not every inflow is a bet on price rising: hedge funds often buy the ETF while shorting CME futures to earn the price gap (a basis trade), which is why ETF inflows and the hedge-fund short in the CFTC panel below often grow together.

CME institutions · CFTC weekly report

Range
GroupLongShortNetNet chg

Net position (long minus short) of each group in Bitcoin or Ether futures on CME, the US exchange where most institutions trade crypto futures. The CFTC (the US futures regulator) collects these positions every Tuesday and publishes them on Friday. Hover the chart to see any week in the table. Big and micro contracts are added together and shown in coins; spread positions (a long and a short held together) are left out. Note: this is CME, not Binance, and it shows who holds positions, not their leverage or entry price. A hedge-fund short is often one leg of a basis trade (buying spot or an ETF while shorting futures to earn the price gap), not a bet on price falling. A sudden one-week jump in one group can also come from the CFTC moving a trader to another group, not only from trading.

What you are looking at

Heatmap (behind the candles): estimated liquidation levels at each moment, from the repo's model v6, a replica of CoinGlass Model 1. Each column shows the levels created during the window before it that price has not touched yet. Brighter = more USD.

Window (how long a level counts): 24 hours up to 1D candles, 7 days on 1W, 30 days on 1M. Only the 24-hour window is checked against CoinGlass; 7 and 30 days use the same rules with levels kept longer. When zoomed out, neighbouring price boxes are added together so each row stays readable.

1D, 1W, 1M go back to 2020: candles and open interest from the Binance public archive (data.binance.vision), joined with this repo's data. The heatmap is calculated with the same model and checked equal to the repo's Python model.

Liquidation map (bars on the right): the same levels for the candle under your cursor, or the latest candle, as a side profile. blue = long positions that get liquidated if price falls to that level; orange = short positions liquidated if price rises to it. Longer bar = more USD.

Lower panes

Actual liquidations: recorded on Binance (Coinalyze feed). Shorts liquidated plot upward, longs liquidated downward. Undercounts the real total, because Binance publishes only the largest liquidation per coin per second.

Open interest: the total size of futures positions still open on Binance, in coins (each contract has a buyer and a seller and is counted once). Rising = new positions opening; falling = positions closing or being liquidated. The heatmap is built from its increases.

Volume: coins traded per candle.

Long vs short: Binance accounts holding a long for every account holding a short (purple, all accounts), and the long/short ratio of Binance's top 20% accounts by margin balance measured by position size (yellow). 1.0 = even. See "Crowd positioning" below for what high values have meant historically.

Coinbase premium: how much higher (above zero) or lower (below zero) bitcoin or ether trades on Coinbase, where US buyers and most US institutions trade, than on Binance spot, in percent, averaged over the candle. Binance prices are in USDT, so they are converted to dollars with Coinbase's USDT price first; otherwise the gap mostly shows USDT trading slightly off $1. Above zero = US buyers are paying up; below zero = US sellers are more eager. It is a reading of behaviour, not a record of who traded. Data: 5-minute values for the last 60 days, daily averages since June 2021 on 1D/1W/1M.

Per candle, on every interval: volume and actual liquidations are totals over the candle; open interest is the value at the candle's close; the heatmap column and the liquidation map show the levels still open at the candle's close (not a sum over the candle).

Data and limits

The heatmap shows where new money entered, not where liquidations are guaranteed. It assumes 25x/50x/100x leverage and isolated margin. Times are WIB (UTC+7); candle buckets follow Binance (UTC).

Source: repo wahyunugi/liqmap-data. The site rebuilds itself after each data collection (every hour); 5m to 4h show the last days, 1D/1W/1M everything since 2020.
